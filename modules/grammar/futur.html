Futur I and Futur IIFutur I und Futur II

1 · The concept

German has no single grammatical future tense. What looks like one is a compound tense built on werden. Futur I joins the present form of werden to a full infinitive, and Futur II adds the infinitive of haben or sein to that whole unit. Both are analytic, both are highly regular, and both are used far more for attitude than for time: Futur I tells you how certain the speaker is, Futur II tells you where the speaker is standing in relation to a future point that has not arrived yet.

Futur I: werden + Infinitiv

Futur I is morphologically simple. The only variable element is the finite part, which behaves exactly like a present-tense verb of any other class, and the only allomorphy is the lost ge- before an infinitive that begins with a vowel. The non-finite infinitive always stands at the very end of the clause, together with any separable prefix, in which case the prefix detaches and slots into the final group.

PersonFutur IExample
ichwerdeIch werde dich morgen anrufen.
duwirstDu wirst dich bestimmt beeilen müssen.
er / sie / eswirdEs wird wahrscheinlich regnen.
wirwerdenWir werden uns nicht beschweren.
ihrwerdetIhr werdet sehen, wie gut das klappt.
sie / SiewerdenSie werden die Regel zuerst lesen.

Word order. In a main clause the finite werde / wirst / wird occupies position two, exactly like any other finite verb, and the infinitive closes the clause. In a subordinate clause the whole unit falls to the end: Ich hoffe, dass ihr die Deadline schönft einhält. Infinitive plus separable prefix also closes the clause, and nothing may follow it except a second infinitive or a noun in a zu-infinitive construction.

Futur I as prediction and assumption

When a German speaker uses Futur I about the future, the tense itself carries a judgement about probability. A bare wird plus infinitive is a hedge, not an assertion. The strength of the hedge is then set by a modal particle – wahrscheinlich, wohl, vermutlich, bestimmt, sicher, offenbar, vielleicht – and by wohl or doch in its own right. The particles sit in second position where the finite verb would otherwise stand, or later if the subject has already claimed that slot, and they are never stressed.

Degree of certaintyRealisation
virtually certainDie Bahn wird auf jeden Fall teurer werden.

probableDer Unfall wird wahrscheinlich teuer.

Futur I also carries presentative force: speaking of a future situation as if it were already under way. Du wirst dich wundern means more than “you will be surprised”; it announces the surprise as an experience the hearer is about to have.

Futur I as promise and intention

The same form covers the speaker’s own commitment, and here the choice of person is a matter of principle. ich werde and wir werden are promises; du wirst and er wird are predictions about someone else, however confident. A promise is often reinforced by a modal particle (Ich werde dich nicht vergessen.), by versprechen with a dass-clause, or simply by a present-tense form of werden used as a full verb, which is the everyday German way to make a firm statement about the future.

Same infinitive, different meaning. werden + infinitive is Futur I, a judgement about the future. Present wird plus infinitive is a neutral future or a plan, and present werden without an infinitive is a full verb: it changes or becomes something, or it stands for a passive. Compare Er wird krank (he will get ill), Er wird alt (he is getting old), Es wird kalt (it is getting cold) and Es wird gebaut (is being built).

Futur II: a past that is still ahead

Futur II is werden + infinitive + haben or sein. It is not a future perfect in the English sense of “by next week I will have done it”. It is a retrospective future: the event lies before the reference point, and the reference point lies in the future. English needs two tenses for this (“by the time X happens, Y will already have happened”); German needs one, because the Futur II participle already says that the action is finished and werden already says that the reference point is not here yet.

Reference pointTranslation patternExample
presentmust be finished by nowEr wird es sicher geschafft haben.
futureby the time + clauseWenn du anrufst, werde ich schon abgereist sein.
future, closedby + dateBis Mai wird der Bau fertiggestellt sein.
future, indefiniteat some point laterMan wird das irgendwann merken.

As a free-standing form, Futur II in the present reference frame does the work of a confident conclusion drawn about something unobserved: the evidence points one way and the speaker commits to it. Er wird das Auto kaputt gefahren haben is “he must have run the car into something”, and a form of sein is required with verbs of motion and change of state: Sie wird im Zug gewesen sein, Er wird dort geblieben sein.

Futur II with time clauses

Whenever a German sentence puts a future event in a subordinate clause and then comments on it from outside, the tense of the comment is Futur II and the clause itself takes the simple preterite – that is, the full form of haben or sein without ge-. German prefers this preterite where English would choose the present perfect or the past perfect, so wenn and als both routinely go with a form such as wäre, hätte, ging, kam rather than with the perfect. The temporal markers that select Futur II in the main clause are bis, bis dahin, bis zu dem Zeitpunkt, zum Zeitpunkt, sobald, wenn, bevor, after and the neutral irgendwann.

PatternExample
bis + main clause Futur IIBis Sonntag wird man den Bericht gelesen haben.
wenn + Präsens, main Futur IWenn ihr die Frist verstreichen lasst, wird es Ärger geben.
sobald + present, main Futur IISobald die Zahlen vorliegen, wird sich das Bild ändern.
zum Zeitpunkt + main Futur IZum Zeitpunkt der Wahl wird sich vieles verschieben.
irgendwann, ohne ZeitangabeMan wird das noch merken.

Note that a genuinely conditional sentence is not a Futur II sentence. In an unreal condition the main clause is Konjunktiv II: Wenn ich das gewusst hätte, hätte ich es dir gesagt. The future reference of a counterfactual is carried by the modal, not by Futur II.

werden + Infinitiv as a substitute for Futur

Neutral statements about the future – schedules, timetables, plans, plain narration – do not need a future form at all. German uses the present tense, and the meaning is carried entirely by the time expression: Der Kurs beginnt im Herbst, Wir fahren morgen nach Berlin. In writing and in speech about arrangements, present werden plus infinitive is the equivalent, Ich werde dich morgen anrufen in its neutral use, and it is a close alternative to Futur I proper. With man the combination is a further idiom for a passive or an impersonal prediction: Man wird sehen. Present werden with no infinitive at all is the standard way to say that something comes about: Die Preise werden teurer, Das wird nicht leicht, Es wird schon wieder kalt.

Three uses, one word. werden + Infinitiv = Futur I. Present werden + Infinitiv = neutral future or plan. Present werden alone = becoming, coming about, or passive. In the third use the participle is a full second predicate and takes zu only in the rare variant with a modal: Das Haus wird abgerissen werden.
