Causal and final connectorsDie kausalen und finalen Konnektoren

1 · The concept

German marks two logical relations with a full range of structures: causality (why something happens) and finality (what something is done for). The decisive question is never the meaning alone — it is always does the second clause have a different subject? and is it a real clause with its own verb position?

The rule in one line. Reason and purpose with the same subject and no special verb go into an infinitive clause (um … zu); everything else needs a real clause with verb-final order (damit, weil).

The four causal mechanisms

TypeMarkerVerb orderExample

weil: position and comma

Weil is completely free in position. It may stand at the front, after any single constituent, or at the end of the sentence. Only position 0 and the middle position take a comma; in final position there is no comma at all.

PositionExampleComma
frontWeil das Wetter schlecht ist, fahren wir nicht.yes
after a subjectWir fahren nicht, weil das Wetter schlecht ist.yes
after a fronted adverbMorgen fahren wir nicht, weil es regnet.yes
endWir fahren nicht, weil das Wetter schlecht ist.no

da: front position only

Da is a weil restricted to the front of the sentence or immediately after a fronted temporal, local or situational element. It never stands at the end and never in position I after the subject.

SlotCorrect or not
position 0Da es regnet, bleiben wir drinnen.  ✓
after a fronted adverbHeute Abend bleiben wir drinnen, da es regnet.  ✓
position I after the subjectWir bleiben da drinnen, weil es regnet.  ✓ — here da is an adverb, weil is the conjunction
end position✗ Wir bleiben drinnen, da es regnet.
position I with weil✗ Wir da bleiben drinnen, weil es regnet.
One reason per sentence. German usually allows only a single weil-clause. A second reason is added with und außerdem, außerdem or zum anderen — not with a second weil.

denn and the alle-Verben exception

Denn is a coordinating conjunction, so it sits in position II and the finite verb stays in position II: Ich bleibe drinnen, denn es regnet.

CaseSentenceVerdict
no strong verbEr arbeitet heute zu Hause, denn das Baby schläft.✓
strong verb in position IIDu musst denn früher gehen.✓
strong verb wrongly displacedDu denn musst früher gehen.✗
The alle-Verben rule. Denn counts as position II. But the verbs sein, haben, werden and all modal verbs always stand in position II and cannot be pushed out. If one of them is in the main clause, it stays in II and denn moves into the field after it.   ✗ Du denn musst gehen.  ✓ Du musst denn früher gehen.

dafür: three shapes

ShapeStructureExample
adverbial reasondafür, dass …Dafür, dass die Bahn ausfiel, kamen wir zu spät.
adverbial reason, no clausedafür + infinitiveDafür sind wir gar nicht zum Sehen gekommen.
adverbial reason, pronoun referencedafür + dafür / + dasDafür hatten wir uns gewundert. / Dafür hängt es.
reference to a purposedafür + infinitiveIch brauche dafür mehr Zeit.
Do not confuse the two uses of dafür. Dafür schläft das Kind, wir gehen spazieren. = for that reason.
Ich stehe jeden Morgen früh auf, damit ich pünktlich bin. — that is the purpose pattern, with damit or um … zu, not with dafür.

Question words built from causal clauses

When a reason is turned into a question, the conjunction disappears. German does not allow warum … weil; the question word alone carries the causal meaning.

Question wordToneQuestionAnswer form
warumneutral, slightly formalWarum bist du gekommen?Weil … / Ich kam, weil …
wiesoneutral, colloquialWieso hast du das vergessen?Weil …
weshalbformal, writtenWeshalb wurde der Kurs abgesagt?Weil …
weswegen / wegen wasformalWeswegen lächst du so?Wegen … / Weil …
Never a conjunction after the question word. ✗ Warum bist du nicht gekommen, weil du krank warst?
✓ Warum bist du nicht gekommen?  /  ✓ Wegen was bist du nicht gekommen?

When um … zu is impossible

An infinitive clause can only be built when the two halves share one subject, the second clause has its own finite verb, and the first verb expresses an intention, a wish or a direction.

Blocked because✗ wrong✓ right
different subjectIch rufe an, um dich informieren.Ich rufe an, damit du Bescheid weißt.
no intentionEr ist krank, um nicht zu arbeiten.Er ist krank, also kann er nicht arbeiten.
modal verb neededIch nehme einen Umweg, um schneller anzukommen.Ich nehme einen Umweg, damit ich schneller ankomme.
modal + subject of the other personIch komme, um dir helfen zu können.Ich komme, damit du mir helfen kannst.
passive or stateEr ruft an, um informiert zu werden.Er ruft an, damit er informiert wird.
Konjunktiv IIEr ruft an, um informiert zu sein.Er ruft an, damit er informiert sei.
Do not park a modal verb in an um … zu clause. A modal verb in the infinitive clause is the clearest signal that you need damit. This holds for können, dürfen, müssen, wollen, sollen. The parallel damit version is always available.

Position of um … zu. The infinitive clause normally stands directly after the verb it belongs to. A long main clause between the two halves is possible but reads heavily, so damit is usually the better choice: Ich habe dir, damit du es weißt, geschrieben.

TimeFormExample

In the perfect tense, um … zu works only with a plain action, not with a modality. …, um Geld abzuheben is fine, because the purpose is a plain action. If the purpose is an ability, a permission or a possibility, the infinitive clause has to be replaced by damit plus a finite modal clause, even after a perfect-tense main verb.

sodass, so … dass and sodann

FormMeaningExample
so …, dassresult, degree named in the main clauseEr war so müde, dass er gleich einschlief.
so …, dassresult, with intensifierDer Wind war so stark, dass die Zweige brachen.
sodassresult, open intensityEs stürmte so stark, sodass der Zug ausfiel.
so … (main clause only)consequence without a clauseEr stolperte, so flägte er die Mappe.
sodann / alsoconsequence in a new sentenceDer Kurs fällt aus. Sodann müssen wir umbuchen.
Sodass is the result of an action, damit the aim of an actor. With verbs like sorgen, machen, bewirken, erreichen the result reading is forced: Er bewirkte, dass der Strom ausfiel. The subject of a sodass-clause is then often impersonal or passive.

Do not confuse these

PairDifference
weil vs. daIdentical grammar, different slot. weil is free; da is reserved for position 0 or after a fronted adverb.
weil vs. dennweil makes a clause and can stand at the end; denn must stay in position II and leaves both finite verbs in position II.
denn vs. dafürdenn is a conjunction in position II; dafür is an adverbial that refers back to a fact already named in the sentence.
um … zu vs. damitSame subject, no Konjunktiv, no modal, no comma → um … zu. Different subject, modal, Konjunktiv or a long gap → damit.
damit vs. sodassdamit is intentional, a purpose someone has; sodass is a consequence that happens, usually unintended.
um … zu vs. sodassLook at the first clause: if it names a result achieved with so, it is sodass, never um … zu.
dafür zu vs. um zuBoth are infinitive clauses. Dafür zu looks back at an already-mentioned reason; um zu states the goal of the same action.
warum vs. weshalbNo grammar difference; wieso and warum are neutral, weshalb and weswegen are written and formal.
A purpose clause is not a result clause, even with the same subject. Ich sprach leise, damit niemand etwas hörte. = deliberate, the speaker lowered his voice.
Ich sprach leise, sodass niemand etwas hörte. = the effect of speaking quietly.
Only the first one can be replaced by um … zu.
